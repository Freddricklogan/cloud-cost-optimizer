# Case Study — Cloud Cost Optimizer

**Repository:** [cloud-cost-optimizer](https://github.com/Freddricklogan/cloud-cost-optimizer) · **Live demo:** [freddricklogan.github.io/cloud-cost-optimizer](https://freddricklogan.github.io/cloud-cost-optimizer/) · **Author:** Freddrick Logan

---

## 1. Who has this problem

IT leaders at universities and public agencies who receive a cloud bill across two or three providers and are asked, by a CFO or a board, what it will be at month end and what could be cut; the engineer who has to answer without a FinOps platform; and students in a cloud architecture course who have seen many cost dashboards and few they could reproduce.

## 2. The problem, as a scenario

A director opens a dashboard on the twentieth of the month. It shows a total, a percentage change, a savings figure and a list of recommendations with dollar amounts. She asks where the percentage came from. The earlier version of this page could not have answered: the change was a string literal, the savings were 28 % of the total, the trend chart was drawn from random numbers on every click, and the six recommendation percentages had no source. It was decoration over thirty-one hand-typed numbers.

## 3. What it costs to leave it alone

A decision made on an unmeasured number is the direct cost — a reservation bought against a "35 % saving" that nobody derived, or a budget conversation anchored on a projection that did not exist. The indirect cost is credibility: once a finance partner finds one constant dressed as a result, every other tile is suspect. For students, a dashboard that cannot be reproduced teaches that FinOps is presentation rather than arithmetic.

## 4. The approach, and the alternative I rejected

I rejected keeping the inventory table and adding sliders; a single cost and utilisation per resource cannot be trended, forecast or flagged. Instead the input is a FOCUS 1.0 export — the FinOps Open Cost and Usage Specification that the major providers now emit — one row per resource per day, with utilisation carried in the specification's extension columns. `src/focus.js` validates every row and lists the rejected ones. `src/analysis.js` computes month-to-date, a month-end projection from a least-squares line over the last 28 days, month-over-month on complete months only, per-service anomaly days beyond three standard deviations of a trailing 30-day mean, and a 14-day run rate per resource. `src/recommend.js` turns that into idle, right-sizing and commitment items whose savings are arithmetic on the run rate and a named assumption; egress and anomalies are observations with no saving attached. The sample is generated by a seeded script and labelled synthetic on the page.

## 5. What the code does today

The page loads the sample — 5,673 rows, 31 resources across AWS, Azure and GCP, 183 days — or a FOCUS CSV you upload, which never leaves the browser. Filter by provider or environment tag. Set a budget and the assumptions: idle threshold, right-size target, and commitment discounts whose defaults are labelled placeholders until you enter a published or negotiated rate. The tiles report month-to-date, the projection with its method, budget use coloured past 90 and 100 %, month-over-month with the months named, identified savings, idle count and anomalous days. Charts show monthly billed against budget with partial months starred, the last 90 days, cost by service and provider, and savings by kind. The recommendation list states each item's basis; the resource table shows run rate, pricing category and utilisation. Recommendations export as CSV and the whole summary as JSON.

## 6. Evidence

Twenty-one Vitest tests cover ingestion of the sample with zero rejected rows, refusal of files missing FOCUS columns, dropped rows with numbered warnings, mixed-currency detection, reconciliation of provider, service and daily groupings to the same total, the least-squares fit against exact data, a flat series projecting to elapsed-day scaling, the complete-month rule, the injected BigQuery and Lambda spikes being flagged with nothing flagged on flat data, size step-down across three naming schemes with `null` at the smallest size, and each recommendation rule including edited assumptions. Statement coverage of the pure modules is 100 %. In headless Chrome the sample produced month-to-date $17,378 on day 20, a projection of $26,157, month-over-month +5.7 % for July to August, 17 recommendations, 4 idle resources and 6 anomalous days, with zero console errors and no horizontal scroll at 1280 or 400 pixels. `AUDIT.md` lists thirteen findings against the earlier build.

## 7. What it would take to run this in production

Point it at a real export — AWS, Azure and Google Cloud billing all offer FOCUS output — and join utilisation from each provider's monitoring service into the `x_UtilizationPct` column with a small job. Replace the placeholder discounts with the provider's published commitment rates for the families in use. For files beyond a few hundred thousand rows the parser should move to a worker with streaming; the analysis is linear in rows and would follow.

## 8. Limits and next steps

The projection is a straight line, which is honest for a month but blind to a step change until 28 days absorb it. Right-sizing assumes price scales linearly within a family and knows only common naming schemes. Storage tiering and spot are not recommended because access patterns and interruption tolerance are not in the data. Next, in order: a seasonal-naive forecast alongside the linear one, unit-cost views by tag, and a diff against the previous export so a review shows what changed rather than what is.

## 9. Who should look at this

**Hiring manager:** evidence that I build cost reporting whose numbers reconcile and whose assumptions are visible, on the open specification the industry is converging on.
**Consulting client:** a way to get a defensible month-end projection and a basis-labelled savings list from the export you already have, before buying a platform.
**Engineer:** read `src/analysis.js` with `tests/analysis.test.js` for the forecast and anomaly rules, and `src/recommend.js` for how each saving is derived.
